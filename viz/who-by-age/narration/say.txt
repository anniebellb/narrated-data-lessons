We all get the same twenty-four hours. But who we spend them with... changes a lot over a lifetime. Let's take a look. People in their thirties and forties are most often with their children. Past sixty-five, it's a partner... or no one at all. [[slnc 800]] Now look at the pink row: friends. Teenagers and people in their early twenties see friends the most. By forty, that time has almost disappeared. [[slnc 800]] Add up every hour of the day, and one pattern stands out. Time alone grows with age, from about five hours a day at twenty, to about eight hours a day at eighty. [[slnc 800]] Experts now call loneliness a public health concern... as serious for our health as smoking. Time alone isn't the same as loneliness... but it's where loneliness can begin. We can't add more hours to the day... but we can choose who we spend them with. A phone call... a shared meal... a short visit. Small moments, together... go a long way.
